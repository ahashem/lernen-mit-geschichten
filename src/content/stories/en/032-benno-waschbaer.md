Once upon a time, there was a little raccoon named Benno. Benno was very curious – maybe too curious. He wanted to try everything without really thinking first.

---

One day, he saw his friend Lilli the duck at the river. Lilli skillfully caught a fish. Benno thought: “That’s easy! I’ll just jump into the water!”

---

Splash! – Benno landed in the water, but the fish swam away. All the animals on the shore laughed a little. Benno shook the water off his fur and smiled.

---

The next day, Benno saw the clever frog Fritz. Fritz was sitting very still on a lily pad. Then – zack! – he snapped up a fly with his tongue.

---

Benno thought: “I can do that too!” He stuck out his tongue, but of course, it didn’t work. The fly buzzed away, and Benno only had a wet mouth.

Then Lilli said: “Benno, you are great because you always try. But do you know the secret? It is: First observe, then think, then act.”

---

Benno scratched his head. He sat down and watched carefully: how Lilli waited patiently, how Fritz stayed still.

---

On the third day, Benno tried again. He watched the fish closely, waited patiently – and zack! – this time it worked. Benno caught his very first fish!

---

All the animals cheered: “Hooray for Benno!”

Benno laughed and said: “I have learned: Whoever observes well, learns twice as much!”

And from that day on, Benno was not only curious, but also a little master of observing.

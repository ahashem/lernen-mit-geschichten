Once upon a time there was a little lion named **Leo**. Leo was very curious and full of energy, but he had a problem: **he didn’t really listen to others**.

---

When his mother said, “Leo, please put your toys away!” Leo simply ran outside and kept playing without listening.

---

When his teacher at school said, “Children, today we’re going to make a paper kite. You’ll need paper, scissors, and glue,” Leo heard only “kite!” and shouted, “Yippee!”—without fetching the other things.

---

One day the school organized a **trip to the forest**. The teacher said, “Children, stay on the path, don’t touch unfamiliar plants, and stay together!” But Leo heard only “forest—exciting!” and ran off into the woods by himself.

---

Suddenly Leo found himself **all alone**. The birds were chirping, the trees were rustling, but there wasn’t another child in sight. Leo felt sad. He sat down on a tree stump and whispered, “I should have listened better . . .”

---

After a while Leo heard footsteps. It was his teacher with the class. “Leo! There you are!” she cried. “I’m sorry,” Leo said. “I wasn’t listening. But I promise: **from now on I’ll listen carefully!**”

---

From that day on, Leo became the **best listener in the class. He raised his hand, waited until others finished speaking, and nodded when he understood. All the children enjoyed talking with Leo because they knew: **Leo really listens.

---

And if you listen carefully, you can hear Leo’s quiet roar as he says, “Listening is power!”

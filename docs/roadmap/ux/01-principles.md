# 01 Principles

Ten principles, in priority order. When two of them conflict, the one higher on the list wins. The
reading-band model they rely on is defined at the end of this file.

---

### 1. The story is the screen

While a story is open, the story's content (picture and words) takes at least 85% of the viewport.
Chrome appears when it is needed and fades out after 3 s without input. Takacs et al. (2015) found
that incidental interactivity costs comprehension, and Hirsh-Pasek et al. name engagement with the
learning goal as a core pillar.

| This means                                                                                                      | This does not mean                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One primary action per screen. In the storybook the only persistent controls are next, previous and play/pause. | Hiding settings. Adult and older-reader controls sit behind one clearly labelled ⋯ menu. |
| No hotspots or tap-for-a-sound on illustrations unless the interaction _is_ the skill (principle 4).            | No interactivity. A breathing page in a calming story is the lesson itself.              |

### 2. Level belongs to the story, not the child

Every story carries a **band** (1-4, defined below). The band sets its reader layout, type scale,
narration default and question style. A child is never assigned a level. A device simply remembers
which shelf to put in front.

| This means                                                                                                                                                    | This does not mean                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| All four shelves are visible and open to everyone, always. A 5-year-old may listen to a Long read with an adult, and an 11-year-old may enjoy a Picture book. | Hiding or locking shelves "for their age".                                                                     |
| Shelf names describe the _format_: Picture books, Read-along, Chapter stories, Long reads.                                                                    | "Level 2", "Ages 6-7", colour belts or caps shown to children. Ages and Klassen appear only in adult metadata. |
| Moving up is offered as a door ("Something longer?"), never as a promotion.                                                                                   | "Level up!", "You're ready for big-kid stories", or ever moving a child _down_ automatically.                  |

### 3. One shell, credible at 11

The shell (home, library, settings, menus) is designed for the oldest reader and for a Klasse 6
teacher. Young-child affordances are _added_ on top: Picture home, large icons and talking faces.
The opposite approach (a toddler shell the older children must tolerate) loses the 9-12-year-olds,
who are quick to reject anything that reads as "for babies".

| This means                                                                                                                                                                                          | This does not mean                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Default home on a fresh device is **Reader home**: text-led, with search and shelves. A large "Picture books" door switches to **Picture home**, and a kita or family device remembers that choice. | Two separate sites, or a "kids/teens" split.                  |
| UI type, icons and copy in the shell are restrained and editorial. Playfulness lives in the illustrations and stories.                                                                              | Cartoon chrome, mascots in the nav, or bubble fonts in menus. |

### 4. Ears first, for those who need them

A pre-reader must be able to start, follow and finish a story without help. Older readers must be
able to switch the voice off and never be talked at.

| This means                                                                                                                                            | This does not mean                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Bands 1-2: faces, covers and the 4 core icons speak their name on tap. Narration is on by default with marker-pen word highlight.                     | Talking on hover, on page load, or anywhere in bands 3-4 unless the reader pressed play. |
| Bands 3-4: narration is available but off by default. For second-language readers it comes back with highlight, slower speed and tap-a-word (see 05). | Treating device TTS as equal to a recorded voice. TTS is labelled as the fallback.       |
| Bands 1-2: a story is 2 taps from Picture home.                                                                                                       | Removing text for young children. Seeing the words while hearing them is the point.      |

### 5. No engagement mechanics, at any age

Lepper et al. (1973) and Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999) show that expected rewards erode intrinsic
interest. Radesky et al. (2022) catalogue the manipulative patterns in children's apps. Older
children are not exempt: points for reading are the norm in German schools (Antolin), and that is
exactly why we do not add more. Any progress system is also a behavioural profile, even one stored
locally.

| This means                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | This does not mean                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Banned:** points, stars, coins, badges, streaks, levels, leaderboards, read counts and totals, "come back tomorrow", auto-play of the next story, countdowns, idle nagging by characters, and any reward promised in advance. | No memory. Allowed: a bookmark ribbon where you stopped, a faint "read before" mark, and for bands 3-4 a **reading list the reader makes** ("Want to read" / "Finished"), shown as a list with no counts. |
| A story ends on a calm closing screen. The next step is a choice.                                                                                                                                                               | A dead end. The end offers talk, an activity, or read again.                                                                                                                                              |
| The take-home activity (colouring page at 3-7, comic, story map or discussion card at 8-12) is the "reward": a real object made off-screen.                                                                                     | Printables as a gimmick. They come from the story's own frames.                                                                                                                                           |

### 6. Talk, don't test

Reflection starts conversations. In bands 1-2 it follows dialogic reading (Whitehurst's CROWD
prompts). In bands 3-4 it becomes perspective-taking, dilemmas and finding evidence in the text.

| This means                                                                                                              | This does not mean                                                                     |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| At most 3 cards in bands 1-2 and at most 5 in bands 3-4. Every set contains at least one card with no "right" answer.   | Scores, ticks and crosses, "4/5", timers, or a quiz gate before the next story.        |
| A wrong recall answer leads back to the story ("Let's look again" / "Find the sentence where…").                        | Pretending all answers are right. Recall has an answer, and we guide the reader to it. |
| Praise names the thinking ("You found the line where Mira changes her mind"), never the person (Mueller & Dweck, 1998). | Silence. Feedback is specific and warm.                                                |

### 7. Two languages, one story

Bilingual families and DaZ classrooms use a home language next to German. Neither is "the
translation".

| This means                                                                                      | This does not mean                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Endonyms (Deutsch, العربية, English, Türkçe, اردو), each in its own script's font.              | Flags.                                           |
| Two-language view gives both languages equal size and contrast, and the reader picks the order. | The second language in grey, smaller, or italic. |
| A missing translation is stated, together with the languages that are available.                | A silent German fallback inside an Arabic UI.    |

### 8. Designed for the grown-up beside the child

Families and teachers get a quiet layer of their own: why this story, what to ask, what to do next.

| This means                                                                                                                                                                                                              | This does not mean                                                                                 |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Every story has a "For grown-ups" panel with the skill, summary, prompts, activity, print pack and Class-room entry. In bands 3-4 it is labelled "For teachers & families", since the reader may be the one opening it. | A parental gate. Nothing here needs protecting. Destructive actions use a standard confirm dialog. |
| Class links let a teacher set shelf and languages on pupil devices in one step (03).                                                                                                                                    | Class accounts, rosters or tracking pupils.                                                        |

### 9. One hand-made world that grows up

There is one illustration language and one cast of recurring characters, drawn with proportions
and detail that mature by band (06). The cast ages with the reader: the same animals appear in a
Long read with older proportions and harder problems.

| This means                                                                   | This does not mean                               |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Every visual comes from the 5 inks plus paper. Each story picks 2 lead inks. | Bands 3-4 switching to a different "cool" style. |
| Character sheets have locked age variants per band.                          | Photorealism, 3D, gradients, or "AI gloss".      |

### 10. Nothing leaves the device; built for the worst device in the room

Static hosting, self-hosted fonts and audio, no analytics, no third-party requests and no accounts.
The baseline devices are a 2019 Android tablet with 2 GB RAM on unreliable wifi and a 4 GB school
Chromebook.

| This means                                                                                                                                                        | This does not mean                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Personal state lives in localStorage or IndexedDB. "Forget this device" clears it all, which matters on shared class devices.                                     | "Anonymous" usage data.                                                                              |
| Budgets: first story in 2.5 s or less on slow 4G, interaction JS under 60 KB gzip per page, fonts per 02, and images lazy-loaded with the next 2 pages preloaded. | Dropping the grain or motion. The grain is an 8 KB tile, and motion uses only transform and opacity. |

## Reading bands

Bands describe stories. The ages are guidance for adults and are never shown to children.

|                        | **Band 1: Picture books**                           | **Band 2: Read-along**                           | **Band 3: Chapter stories**                                    | **Band 4: Long reads**                                                         |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Typical reader         | 3-5, kita, pre-reader, listens and looks            | 6-7, Klasse 1-2, emerging reader                 | 8-9, Klasse 3-4, independent reader                            | 10-12, Klasse 5-6, confident reader                                            |
| Length                 | 6-10 spreads, 150-400 words                         | 8-14 pages, 300-800 words                        | 3-6 short chapters, 1,200-3,000 words                          | 4-10 chapters, 3,000-8,000 words                                               |
| Text per view          | ≤ 3 short sentences                                 | ≤ 5 sentences                                    | ~120-250 words per page                                        | Continuous chapter text                                                        |
| Picture density        | 1 full frame per spread; picture carries the story  | 1 frame per page; picture supports decoding      | 1 frame per 2-3 pages + chapter opener                         | 1 opener per chapter, optional comic-panel sequences                           |
| Narration              | On by default, auto-start, word highlight           | On by default, word highlight, tap-a-word        | Off by default, available, highlight optional                  | Off by default; "Listen" mode; L2 support tools                                |
| Reader layout          | Storybook (paged spreads)                           | Storybook                                        | Chapter reader (paged on tablet, scroll on phone)              | Chapter reader                                                                 |
| Navigation             | Icon-led (Picture home)                             | Icon-led with text labels                        | Text-led (Reader home)                                         | Text-led                                                                       |
| Tap target             | 64 px                                               | 56 px                                            | 48 px                                                          | 44 px min (48 default)                                                         |
| Story type (Latin)     | 30/24 px                                            | 28/24 px                                         | 21 px                                                          | 19 px                                                                          |
| Motion and sound       | Gentle page turn, spoken labels, optional music bed | Same, quieter                                    | Minimal; no UI sounds                                          | Minimal; no UI sounds                                                          |
| Reflection             | Picture recall, feeling faces, talk card            | + simple word choices                            | Text choices, "what would you do?", find-the-sentence          | Perspective cards, dilemmas, evidence from the text, discussion prompts        |
| Take-home              | Colouring page, thick lines                         | Colouring page + draw-the-ending                 | Detailed colouring, story map, comic with empty speech bubbles | Detailed pattern or comic pages, character perspective sheet, discussion cards |
| Themes (examples only) | naming feelings, calming down, sharing, bedtime     | patience, saying sorry, being brave, new sibling | friendship conflicts, fairness, handling mistakes, teamwork    | peer pressure, belonging, online behaviour, standing up for someone            |

### Who chooses, and how it is remembered

- **Adult or teacher:** Settings → "Who reads on this device?" offers Picture books / Read-along /
  Chapter stories / Long reads / **Mixed (family or kita)**. Mixed puts Picture home first and shows
  all shelves.
- **Teacher, at scale:** a class link or QR code on a prep sheet, e.g.
  `…/?shelf=chapter&lang=de&second=tr`. On open it shows "This device will start with Chapter
  stories in Deutsch, with Türkçe alongside. OK?". Nothing is applied silently.
- **Child:** may pick any shelf at any time. The choice reorders home and is remembered.
- **Storage:** one key, e.g. `reader-prefs` =
  `{ shelf, home: 'picture'|'reader', lang, second, textSize, narration, theme }`. There is no
  history of what was read beyond the bookmark and the optional reading list.

### Moving up without labels

- Each shelf view ends with **one story from the next shelf**, marked only by its format ("A
  chapter story, 3 chapters").
- When a reader finishes a story with narration off (bands 1-2) or reaches the last chapter (band
  3), the end screen may add one quiet tile: "Something longer?" It is never repeated within the
  session and never animated.
- The device does not track a reading level or move a child automatically, and it never moves one
  down.

## Voice and tone

### Child voice, by band

| Band | Who speaks                                | How                                                                                       | Example: wrong recall answer                                                 |
| ---- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | The narrator, or the characters           | ≤ 8-word sentences, present tense, concrete nouns, warm                                   | "Let's look again. Where is Bruno's red balloon?"                            |
| 2    | The narrator                              | ≤ 12 words, simple past is fine, names feelings precisely                                 | "Look back at this page. What did Bruno hold?"                               |
| 3    | A neutral, friendly voice addressing "du" | Direct, light humour allowed, explains why                                                | "Not quite. Find the sentence where Mira notices the note."                  |
| 4    | Peer-respectful                           | No diminutives, no exclamation marks, acknowledges complexity, never spells out the moral | "Look at chapter 3 again. What does Jonas say right after the group laughs?" |

For every band, **never** use guilt ("Bruno is sad you left"), urgency ("Quick!"), comparison
("Other kids…"), or a moral delivered by the UI ("Remember: sharing is caring!"). In bands 3-4, the
story shows and the reader decides.

Address: informal "du" (de), "sen" (tr), "tum" (ur), and the singular in Arabic (see 07 on gendered
address).

### Adult voice

- Peer to peer. For families: "Sie" in German UI. Teacher materials use school vocabulary
  (Klasse, DaZ, Gesprächskreis, Arbeitsblatt).
- Lead with what to _do_: "Ask: what made Bruno's tummy feel tight?"
- Be honest: "Illustrations are made with AI tools and checked by our team."
- No superlatives or promised outcomes.

### Moderator voice

Imperative checklists with an example per rule and no jargon ("story details", not
"frontmatter"). The band is a required field with a one-line description of each band next to it.
